# Phase 4 review: Portfolio construction

Written on 2026-11-17 at the end of the phase. The numbers below are quoted from the generated reports, [`reports/phase-04/portfolio.md`](../../../reports/phase-04/portfolio.md) and [`reports/phase-04/model_b.md`](../../../reports/phase-04/model_b.md), and the log, [`reports/phase-04/experiments.jsonl`](../../../reports/phase-04/experiments.jsonl), built from the phase 0 and 1 tables of the pinned snapshot and Model B's table, with no new download. Every path is filled at the open of *t+1*, from 2017-01-03 to 2026-11-13, with returns per dollar of capital and phase 3's headline cost model. "The optimiser" is the headline: Model A's signal and risk, λ = 30, κ = 1, no turnover budget, a minimum trade of $1,000 and no re-solve. "The decile sort" is phase 3's baseline path at the same cost.

## What was built

- **`src/statarb/factors/pca.py`**: Model B, the second `FactorModel`, after Avellaneda and Lee (2010), section 2.1: the top 15 eigenvectors of the correlation matrix of the stocks with a complete 252-day window ending *t−1*, eigenportfolios weighted *v*/σ, and each stock with at least 189 returns regressed on them. `statarb.factors.build --model pca` writes `residuals_pca.parquet` beside Model A's table and a report comparing the two (`statarb.factors.compare`).
- **`src/statarb/portfolio/`**: the risk models (`RiskModel`: Model A's ETF factors or Model B's eigenportfolios, each with trailing specific variance), the optimiser and the decile sort, both engine policies behind the `Policy` seam, the paths of the brief, the runner `statarb.portfolio.run` and its report, from `config/portfolio.toml`. The optimiser is stated in cvxpy and solved by Clarabel with its tolerances in config; anything but an optimal solution holds the previous book.
- **The engine's `Policy`** now also sees the holdings after the previous fill and returns a note for the day, which the optimiser uses to hold its book on a failed day and to record each solve. The no-trade zone's decisions are unchanged.
- **Libraries:** SciPy, cvxpy and Clarabel, pinned exactly. SciPy's wheel bundles the GCC runtime library under GPL-3.0-or-later with the GCC Runtime Library Exception, which leaves the repository's MIT licence intact ([architecture](../../architecture.md#external-libraries)).
- **Solve times** are wall-clock and vary from run to run, so they are printed with the runner's progress and recorded in [requirements](../../requirements.md), not in the report or the log, which must rebuild byte for byte. The brief listed them among the report's contents.
- **Tests**: Model B's eigenvalues against the eigenportfolios' variances, partial histories and look-ahead; constraints on random inputs; the turnover budget never exceeded; the minimum trade filter and the re-solve's neutrality; a forced solver failure holding the book; a sort from the optimiser with no cost and no risk; look-ahead for the risk models, the calibration and the decisions; exact reproduction; strict config keys; a byte-identical report. `tests/contract/test_portfolio.py` runs the decile sort and the optimiser through the same checks.

The choices the drafted brief left open are settled in [its revision](brief.md#revised-at-the-start-of-the-phase). The rule there set the headline λ before any backtest return was seen: over the first 60 days with an expected move, the book deployed on average 0.888 of its capital at $500k and 0.859 at $5m with λ = 30, and 0.263 and 0.361 with λ = 100, so 30 is the largest candidate that deploys 70% at both sizes.

## What the numbers showed

- **No path keeps a net edge.** The best net Sharpe of any path is phase 3's three-day lookback decile sort at $5m, 0.28. The best optimiser path is the three-day lookback at $5m, 0.22 with a t-statistic of 0.70 and a deflated Sharpe probability of 0.201 against a benchmark Sharpe of 0.50. Only two other pairs of optimiser path and book size are above zero: κ = 2 at $5m, 0.03, and the three-day lookback at $500k, 0.01.
- **The optimiser raises gross Sharpe and loses it to trading.** At $500k its gross Sharpe is 0.85 against the decile sort's 0.71, and its gross return 4.04% against 3.60%, but it trades 0.685 of capital a day against 0.349 and pays 8.31% a year in costs against 7.60%, so its net Sharpe is −0.96 against −0.85. At $5m the gap is wider, −0.49 against −0.11, with impact at 2.83% a year against 1.37%.
- **It is neutral.** Beside the exposures in the acceptance table, the fitted (market and sector) part of the book's returns has a volatility of 0.73% a year at $500k against the decile sort's 1.98%, and the mean dollar imbalance after the minimum trade filter is 0.0149 of gross at $500k and 0.0009 at $5m.
- **Trading less helps, and the cost penalty's κ decides how much.** Net Sharpe rises steadily with κ: −1.58, −0.96 and −0.36 at κ = 0.5, 1 and 2 at $500k, and −1.08, −0.49 and 0.03 at $5m. It also rises as the turnover budget tightens, from −0.75 at *τ* = 100% to −0.26 at 10% at $500k. λ matters less, and not in one direction: at $500k λ = 10 nets −0.67 and λ = 100 −1.11 against the headline's −0.96, and at $5m the three are within 0.06 of each other.
- **The minimum trade size trades commission for neutrality.** At $500k a minimum of $0 is neutral but pays 6.79% a year in commission, net Sharpe −1.44; $1,000 cuts commission to 4.42% and leaves dollar or factor breaches above 0.1% of capital on 2,137 and 1,784 of 2,480 days; $5,000 cuts it to 1.36% and nets −0.40, but deploys 0.32 of capital in a median of 20 names, with dollar breaches up to 0.175 of capital.
- **The re-solve buys back neutrality at $5m and only part of it at $500k, and changes no result.** At $5m with a $1,000 minimum it cuts dollar breach days from 206 to 9; at $500k from 2,137 to 790, with 703 days on which a re-solve failed and the filtered book was kept. Net Sharpe moves by at most 0.06.
- **Model B as a residual.** Its residuals are smaller than Model A's (standard deviation 0.0156 against 0.0170 on member rows) and correlated 0.859 with them ([Model B report](../../../reports/phase-04/model_b.md)). The decile sort on them earns 2.30% gross against 3.60% and nets −1.61 at $500k against −0.85. By size bucket its gross P&L per dollar of gross is lower in every bucket but the third, and the largest two buckets still earn most of it, 1.01% and 1.77% a year against Model A's 2.12% and 2.28%.
- **Model B as a risk model.** With Model A's signal, Model B's risk does worse than Model A's at both sizes, −1.34 against −0.96 at $500k, at the same turnover, and leaves a mean absolute exposure to Model A's sector factors of 0.160, which Model A's risk does not constrain away. Model B for both has the highest gross Sharpe of any optimiser path at $500k, 1.21, and deploys 0.52 of capital; its net Sharpe is −1.04.
- **Against the no-trade zone.** Phase 3's zone on the same signal and book has a higher net Sharpe in all four comparisons, 0.23 against −0.96 for the baseline at $500k and 0.54 against 0.22 for the three-day lookback at $5m, by trading an eighth to a half as much and deploying less capital, with a dollar imbalance of 0.14 to 0.26 of gross against the optimiser's 0.001 to 0.015.
- **The spread bracket changes no verdict.** Nothing clears the bar at any half-spread from 1 to 5 bps. The three-day lookback through the optimiser changes sign within it: 0.10 at 1 bps and −0.19 at 2.5 bps at $500k, and 0.05 at 2.5 bps and −0.39 at 5 bps at $5m.
- **The solver.** 2,480 decision days for each optimiser path; 68 idle before the expected move exists (131 for Model B's signal, 70 for the three-day lookback); one failed day across every path, for *τ* = 25% at $500k, on which the previous book was held. A day solves in well under a second; the times are in [requirements](../../requirements.md).

## What surprised us

- **The optimiser's gain is a 2020 result.** The optimiser's best result is the three-day lookback, the only optimiser path with a net return above zero at both book sizes, and at $500k it beats its decile sort, 0.01 against −0.24. It earns 31.19% of capital in 2020 and loses in eight of the other nine years ("Net return by year"); at $5m the same path earns 29.09% in 2020 and again loses in eight of the other nine. The headline optimiser's best year is also 2020, 10.20% at $500k, the only year it gains at that size. A pooled Sharpe over ten years can be one year; the review reads the year table before the pooled one, and phase 5's comparison of the model with the baseline has to do the same.
- **Neutrality removed risk, not losses.** The constraints did what they were for: market beta, sector exposure and the volatility of the fitted part of the book's returns all fell to a fraction of the decile sort's. But the decile sort's fitted P&L was small to begin with, 0.22% a year of capital, and most of the book's volatility is residual, so total volatility fell only from 5.09% to 4.71% at $500k. The losses come from trading costs on a small gross edge, and the optimiser, which trades more, made them larger. Phase 3's zone gave up neutrality and lost less; neutrality is worth its cost only if the exposure it removes was costing something.
- **The cost term at κ = 1 under-prices trading.** κ = 1 charges each trade exactly what the cost model charges, so it should be the right weight if the expected moves were right. That net results improve monotonically up to κ = 2 says the expected moves overstate what a trade earns, or that a one-period problem cannot see that today's trade is reversed within days. Either way the optimiser trades more than its forecasts can pay for.

## Review questions

**Does Model B change the picture, as a residual or as a risk model?** No. As a residual it gives a smaller, steadier book with less gross return; as a risk model it does worse than Model A with the same signal. No verdict changes, and Model B for both is not better net than the headline at either size.

**Where does the improvement come from: lower turnover, better neutrality, or better use of forecast strength?** There is no net improvement at the headline. The gross improvement, 0.85 against 0.71 in gross Sharpe at $500k, comes from neutrality and forecast weighting together, and is spent on turnover twice the decile sort's. Within the optimiser every net improvement comes from trading less.

**How sensitive are results to the cost multiplier?** Very: net Sharpe moves by more than 1.1 from κ = 0.5 to 2 at both sizes, monotonically, with the best at the edge of the sweep. The headline, set before any result, is not where the optimiser does best, and any κ chosen after seeing this table is another trial.

**At matched turnover, does the cost penalty or the turnover budget give better net results, and which is less sensitive to its parameter?** The cost penalty: κ = 2 at $5m trades 0.281 of capital a day and nets 0.03, between *τ* = 25% (0.189, −0.20) and *τ* = 50% (0.325, −0.28), and earns more gross per dollar traded, 4.86 bps against 3.26 and 3.04. At $500k κ = 2 (0.405, −0.36) beats *τ* = 50% (0.343, −0.56). The budget is less sensitive: across *τ* from 10% to 100% net Sharpe moves by 0.49 at $500k and 0.22 at $5m, against 1.22 and 1.11 across κ.

**What does the minimum trade size cost in neutrality, and does the re-solve buy it back?** At $500k a $1,000 minimum leaves dollar breaches on most days, up to 0.0415 of capital; $5,000 breaches up to 0.175 while deploying a third of the capital. At $5m the cost is small, breaches up to 0.0031 on 206 days. The re-solve buys it back at $5m and partly at $500k, where it often cannot find a feasible book once the filtered names are fixed, and it changes no net result.

**Does the optimiser beat the simple no-trade zone by enough to justify its complexity?** No. The zone has a higher net Sharpe in every comparison, at the price of an unbalanced book. The optimiser's case is neutrality and control: it holds a balanced book with exposures near zero and a known capital deployed, which phase 6's paper trade needs. On returns it has not earned its complexity.

**Does any configuration's verdict against the bar change inside the phase 3 spread bracket?** No. The three-day lookback through the optimiser changes sign at both sizes, but no path clears the bar at any half-spread.

## Lessons this phase built in

Six rows added to [`docs/lessons.md`](../../lessons.md). Two have their episodes above: an improvement is read by year before it is believed, since the optimiser's gain is one year; and neutrality is judged by the P&L of what it removes as well as the exposure, since here it removed risk, not losses. The others are rules built in with a test from the start: a failed solve holds the previous book and never relaxes a constraint; a tuning parameter's headline value is set by a rule on ex-ante properties before any return is seen; Model B's residuals up to a day do not change with data after it; and every portfolio construction passes the same contract tests.

## What carries forward

- **To phase 5:** the optimiser at its headline settings, λ = 30, κ = 1, no turnover budget and a $1,000 minimum trade, with Model A for residuals and risk, as the pipeline both the model's forecast and the baseline go through. κ's sensitivity is a finding, not a choice: a different κ is a new trial, chosen inside the walk-forward training window as the phase 5 brief says. The three-day lookback through the optimiser, the best path here, is the benchmark to beat beside the baseline, read by year.
- **The calibration.** The expected move is phase 3's pooled regression over five days. That κ = 1 under-prices trading suggests it overstates what a trade earns; a model forecast enters through the same calibration and inherits the same question.
- **Cost of a run.** An optimiser path takes about a hundred seconds on the full history ([requirements](../../requirements.md)); walk-forward forecasts through it multiply that.
- **The open fill** remains a lower bound on impact, and **survivorship** stays as stated in phase 0, unmeasured.
- **The dependency-upgrade criterion** from phase 0 is still untested: this phase added libraries without moving any existing pin.
