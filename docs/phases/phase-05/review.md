# Phase 5 review: ML signal

Written on 2026-11-17 at the end of the phase. The numbers below are quoted from the generated reports, [`reports/phase-05/ml.md`](../../../reports/phase-05/ml.md) for the development years and [`reports/phase-05/holdout.md`](../../../reports/phase-05/holdout.md) for the holdout, and the log, [`reports/phase-05/experiments.jsonl`](../../../reports/phase-05/experiments.jsonl), built from the phase 0 and 1 tables of the pinned snapshot with no new download. Development paths run from 2020-07-09, the first day every forecast has a calibrated move, to 2025-11-13; the holdout runs from 2025-11-14 to 2026-11-13. Every path is filled at the open of *t+1*, with returns per dollar of capital and phase 3's headline cost model. "The optimiser" is phase 4's headline: λ = 30, κ = 1, no turnover budget, a $1,000 minimum trade, Model A for residuals and risk. "The four forecasts" are the model at *H* = 1 (the headline) and *H* = 3, and the phase 3 variants: the baseline (*n* = 1) and the three-day lookback.

## What was built

- **`src/statarb/models/`**: fifteen features computed from the processed tables in each run (`features`); the ranked target; the walk-forward splitter, with the purge and a 5-day embargo (`splitter`); LightGBM behind the `Backend` interface, selected by name in `config/models.toml` (`backend`, `lgbm`); the per-block grid search and refit (`training`); the forecast registered as a `Signal` (`forecast`); the IC analysis (`ic`); the development runner `statarb.models.run`, its report, and the holdout runner `statarb.models.holdout`.
- **`src/statarb/portfolio/partial.py`**: partial adjustment, the fourth rule, added after the development run by [decision record 0006](../../decisions/0006-partial-adjustment-in-phase-5.md). Each day it trades a fraction θ of the way to the optimiser's solution with no cost term, then applies the minimum-trade filter. θ is chosen from a grid for each forecast and book size so the path's turnover matches the optimiser's, reading turnovers only.
- **The row-removal test.** The look-ahead contract in `tests/contract/test_signals.py` now also builds every registered signal from tables cut at a date, with a member whose residuals start after it, and requires the same scores up to the cut; `tests/test_features.py` does the same for the features. It is told [below](#the-look-ahead-perturbation-cannot-see).
- **Library:** LightGBM 4.7.0, MIT, pinned exactly; its macOS wheel needs Homebrew's `libomp`, the system-runtime exception recorded in `CLAUDE.md`. SHAP values come from LightGBM's own TreeSHAP, so there is no `shap` package.
- **Two development runs and one holdout read.** The first development run (26 entries, commit `447f85e`) had three rules; the second (34 entries, `b41ac84`) added partial adjustment, retrained every model with the same SHA-256 and reproduced every earlier path's metrics. The holdout read (34 entries, `c48ac09`) retrained every development model, found each with its logged SHA-256, and ran every path over the holdout year. Every entry names a clean tree, no overlay and the Alpaca snapshot of 2026-11-16.
- **A fix before the holdout report was committed.** The first holdout report judged both book sizes against one trial set, the $5m one, because book size is not part of a path's id. The development run judges each book against its own set. The fix (`123972e`) rebuilds each book's set from the development run's entries, with a test that it equals the development run's; the logged read holds no verdict, so it stands, and the report was rebuilt from it with `--repeat`, which reproduced every entry. At $500k the benchmark Sharpe moved from 0.62 to 0.63, and the probabilities with it; no verdict changed.
- **Tests**: no train-test overlap after the purge, and both sides of the purge and the embargo; features unchanged when values after *t* are perturbed or rows after it removed; the forecast's calibration on *t* unchanged by later data; byte-identical models from a rebuild, in the backend contract; partial adjustment's aim independent of the book held, and its rate matched to the optimiser's turnover over the whole grid; each runner's refusals; both runners end to end on the synthetic fixture, with byte-identical reports.

The choices the drafted brief left open are settled in [its revision](brief.md#revised-at-the-start-of-the-phase), before any model was trained.

## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| Out-of-sample IC reported with a t-statistic, by year | Met: "Information coefficient" in `ml.md`, by forecast, target horizon, year and decay day, and by block in `holdout.md`. |
| The model is compared with the baseline on net performance through the same optimiser and cost model, not on IC alone | Met: every forecast through the decile sort, the no-trade zone, the optimiser and partial adjustment at both book sizes, over the same dates, with net return by year and the verdict against the bar. |

## What the numbers showed

- **The model forecasts out of sample, about as well as the baseline.** Mean daily IC against the 1-day target is 0.0149 (t 4.4) for the model at *H* = 1 and 0.0175 (t 4.8) at *H* = 3, against the baseline's 0.0185 (t 4.7) and the three-day lookback's 0.0133 (t 3.2). At three and five days the *H* = 3 model has the highest IC of the four, 0.0215 (t 6.3) and 0.0211 (t 6.5). Every forecast's IC is near zero in 2022, and both models' are positive in every other year. These are inside the brief's range for a realistic result, nowhere near the 0.1 that would mean a bug.
- **No model path beats the three-day lookback through the optimiser.** Net Sharpe through the optimiser at $5m is 0.37 for the three-day lookback, −0.22 for the *H* = 3 model, −0.60 for *H* = 1 and −0.98 for the baseline; at $500k 0.09, −0.64, −1.07 and −1.59. The model beats the baseline through the optimiser by trading less, not by earning more: at $500k the *H* = 3 model trades 0.423 of capital a day against the baseline's 0.859, and earns 2.97% gross a year against 3.90%.
- **Nothing clears the bar.** Against 177 trials, the benchmark Sharpe is 0.63 at $500k and 0.62 at $5m; the best deflated-Sharpe probability is the three-day lookback through the optimiser at $5m, 0.285, with a t-statistic of 0.86.
- **Partial adjustment keeps less than the optimiser at the same turnover.** θ matched each optimiser path's turnover to within about 1%, at 0.38 to 0.70. Its gross return is lower for every forecast at both books, and so is its net Sharpe: −0.36 against 0.37 for the three-day lookback at $5m, and −0.44 against −0.22 for the *H* = 3 model ("Partial adjustment at matched turnover").
- **The calibration is positive most days and small.** The trailing slope is positive on 82% to 99% of path days, 99.3% for the *H* = 3 model, and gives a member a mean expected 5-day move of 3.3 bps (*H* = 1) to 7.6 bps (three-day lookback). Phase 4's question, whether the pooled 5-day calibration overstates what a trade earns, carries over unchanged: every optimiser path but the three-day lookback's still pays more in cost than it earns gross.
- **The dispersion defect, measured.** Counted over the whole stock axis instead of the members known on the day, the dispersion feature differs on 1,402 of 2,421 development days, by a median of 0.32 bps (0.2% of the correct value) and at most 103.77 bps (42.5%); on 129 days only the defective definition has a value ("Dispersion over the whole stock axis").

## The holdout

Read once, at commit `c48ac09`, after the configuration was fixed and logged, and rebuilt since only with `--repeat`. It is clean for the model only: phases 2 to 4 ran through 2026-11-13 and chose the baseline, the three-day lookback, the zone and the optimiser's headline with this year in their sample.

- **The IC is gone.** Mean daily IC against the 1-day target is 0.0025 for the baseline, 0.0049 for the three-day lookback, 0.0062 for the *H* = 1 model and 0.0033 for *H* = 3, none with a t-statistic above 0.7, and no forecast's IC at 1, 3 or 5 days reaches t 1.5. By block, the models' IC is negative or near zero until the last block, from 2026-08-19, where it is 0.0352 (t 2.3) at *H* = 1 and 0.0343 (t 2.6) at *H* = 3; the *H* = 3 model's third block is −0.0301 (t −2.9), from a model of one tree.
- **Every optimiser path loses.** Net Sharpe through the optimiser at $5m is −1.74 for the baseline and the three-day lookback, −2.74 for the *H* = 1 model and −0.13 for *H* = 3; at $500k −2.37, −2.06, −3.17 and −0.44. Partial adjustment is below the optimiser for every forecast at both books, at the development θ.
- **The only positive net Sharpes are decile sorts at $5m:** 0.31 for the three-day lookback, 0.16 for the *H* = 3 model and 0.09 for *H* = 1, none with a t-statistic above 0.31. Nothing clears the bar.
- **The *H* = 3 model's optimiser path did no worse than in development because it traded less.** At $500k it traded 0.138 of capital a day against 0.423 in development, and every optimiser path traded between three and seven tenths of its development turnover. That is what the trailing calibration should do when the IC falls, though the holdout report does not measure the calibration.

## What surprised us

- **A better forecast earned less.** By IC the *H* = 3 model is the best forecast of the four: the highest t-statistic at every target horizon, and the highest mean IC at three and five days. Through every rule, at both book sizes, it earns less gross than both phase 3 variants: 2.01% a year through the decile sort against the baseline's 2.98% and the three-day lookback's 4.55%, 2.97% through the optimiser at $500k against 3.90% and 7.32%, and a gross loss through the no-trade zone where both variants earn. The three-day lookback, with the lowest 1-day IC (0.0133), earns the most gross through every rule. A likely reason, which this phase did not measure: IC is a rank correlation that weighs every member-day the same, and the model's target was ranked too, so it was trained to order members, not to find the moves large enough to pay for a trade, while a decile book and an optimiser earn on the size of the moves at the ends. The *H* = 3 model nets better than the baseline only by trading less. Phase 2's lesson, that a statistic about returns is checked in P&L before it decides a design, applied to the forecast itself: the comparison the brief required, through the same rules and costs, is the one that decided it.
- **Reversal after event moves was stronger, not weaker.** The brief expected weaker reversal after high-volume moves. On test-block member-days in the most negative quintile of the day's residual, the realised ranked target is 0.0231 on event days against 0.0064 on others, and in the most positive quintile −0.0292 against −0.0074; the model's forecast follows the same pattern, at a third to a half of the realised size. Event days are few, 2,504 and 2,118 member-days in those quintiles, so this is a pooled result, not one read by year.
- **The holdout is a second 2022.** Every forecast's IC was near zero in 2022; for both models and the baseline it was the only such development year. The holdout year looks like 2022 for every forecast, the phase 3 variants included, so it says more about reversal in that year than about the model.

## The look-ahead perturbation cannot see

The perturbation test, built in phase 2, changes every value after a cut-off and requires nothing before it to change. It cannot see a value that depends on whether a later row exists, since it leaves which rows exist alone. The signal tables' stock axis is every stock with a residual anywhere in the loaded tables, so a stock whose residuals start later is on the axis from the first day, and a cross-section over the axis counts it on days a live run would not know it.

The dispersion feature is the clearest case. Its obvious definition, the cross-sectional spread of members' total returns over the stock axis, counts members before their first residual, but only those that have a residual later. It passes the perturbation test. The row-removal test builds the feature from tables cut at a date, with a member whose residuals start after the cut, and from the whole tables, and requires the same values up to the cut; it fails that definition, and `tests/test_features.py` keeps the definition as a known defect to show that it does. The feature counts only the members with a residual on or before the day. On the development data the defective definition would have differed on 1,402 of 2,421 days, by a median of 0.32 bps and at most 103.77 bps, 42.5% of the correct value. The development runner measures this as a statistic of the feature; no model was trained or backtested on it, so it adds no trial and has no P&L.

When the same test was run over every registered signal, it failed two from phase 2: the raw reversal signal, which scores a member from its total returns before its first residual, and the s-score, which fits such members and centres across them. On the pinned snapshot that touches 2,362 member-days of 25 stocks on 1,349 days, from 2017-01-03. It reaches phase 2's and phase 3's raw control and s-score paths, not the residual signal, the model or any phase 4 or 5 path. Both are marked as strict expected failures that name the defect, and the fix is moved into the phase 6 brief ([backlog walk](#backlog-walk)).

## Review questions

**Does the model learn interpretable rules, such as weaker reversal after high-volume or earnings moves?** Partly. The *H* = 1 model's largest out-of-sample SHAP values are the 1- and 3-day residual sums, the reversal itself, followed by the distance from the 20-day low and the volume ratio. Dispersion and SPY's volatility have the largest gain shares, 11.4% and 10.7%, but are tenth and eleventh of fifteen by SHAP, which suggests the trees use them to split the sample into regimes rather than as forecasts in their own right. Reversal after event moves is stronger, not weaker, and the model has learnt that direction ([above](#what-surprised-us)).

**Does the higher IC survive costs once the optimiser controls turnover?** The model's IC is not higher at the headline horizon: at one day the baseline's is higher than either model's. The *H* = 3 model's higher IC at three and five days does not survive: through the optimiser it earns less gross than both variants, and nets −0.64 at $500k and −0.22 at $5m against the three-day lookback's 0.09 and 0.37.

**How much did the final holdout year differ from the walk-forward average?** By far. The development 1-day IC was 0.0133 to 0.0185 with t-statistics of 3.2 to 4.8; in the holdout it is 0.0025 to 0.0062, with none above t 0.7. Every optimiser path's net Sharpe is lower than in development except the *H* = 3 model's, which traded a third as much. The holdout is one year, and its IC resembles 2022's, the one development year in which neither model nor the baseline had any.

**Does the model beat the three-day lookback through the optimiser in more years than it loses?** Only by losing less. By net return, the *H* = 1 model beats it in four of six development years at $500k and two at $5m; the *H* = 3 model in three at each. Every year either model beats it is a year both lose, and the three-day lookback's two large gains, 2020 and 2023, are years it beats both models at both sizes. In the holdout the *H* = 3 model loses less at both sizes (−0.87% against −8.98% at $500k), and the *H* = 1 model about the same at $500k and more at $5m.

**At matched turnover, does partial adjustment keep more of the gross than the one-period optimiser, and for which forecasts?** No forecast. At turnover matched to within about 1%, partial adjustment's gross return is lower for all four forecasts at both books, by 0.7 to 3.4 percentage points a year, and its net Sharpe lower by 0.22 to 1.33. In the holdout, at the development θ, it is below the optimiser everywhere again.

## Lessons this phase built in

Four rows in [`docs/lessons.md`](../../lessons.md). Two have their episodes above: the look-ahead test also removes the rows after a cut-off, since perturbing values cannot see a value that depends on whether a later row exists; and a forecast is judged by its P&L through the same rules and costs, not by its IC, since the forecast with the best IC earned less than both simpler ones. The others are rules built in with a test from the start: the model library sits behind a small interface whose every implementation passes the same checks, a byte-identical rebuild among them; and runners stop at the development end, with the holdout read once and adding no trial.

## What carries forward

- **No forecast has a net edge, and the holdout is spent.** No path in any phase clears the bar, and the holdout year has been read. Any later change to the model or the rules is chosen with every year in sight; from here the only out-of-sample data is what phase 6 collects forward.
- **To phase 6:** the brief runs "the chosen strategy" forward. Nothing here chooses one on returns. On development data the three-day lookback through the optimiser at $5m is the best path, and in the holdout the *H* = 3 model through the optimiser loses least; phase 6's revision picks the forward strategy by a rule stated before any forward day is seen, and its purpose is to test that the live pipeline matches the backtest, which any of them can do. The model files are LightGBM text with logged SHA-256 values, which the daily job's hash check needs.
- **The calibration.** The trailing 5-day calibration shrank the optimiser's trading in the holdout as the IC fell, as it should; whether it overstates what a trade earns is still open, as phase 4 left it.
- **The open fill** remains a lower bound on impact, and **survivorship** stays as stated in phase 0, unmeasured.
- **The dependency-upgrade criterion** from phase 0 is still untested: LightGBM entered without moving an existing pin.
